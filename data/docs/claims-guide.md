# Understanding Your Claims
This guide explains how claims work. All content is fictional and used for a prototype.

## What is a claim
A claim is a request for payment that your provider sends to the plan after you receive care.

## Claim statuses
A claim moves through defined statuses. Received means the plan has the claim and has not started the review. In Review means the plan is checking the claim against your benefits. Pending Information means the plan needs more details from you or your provider. Processed - Paid means the plan has paid its share. Processed - Denied means the plan did not pay the claim.

## How long a claim takes
Most claims are processed within 30 calendar days of receipt. If a claim is pending information, the 30 days are paused until the plan receives the missing details.

## Explanation of Benefits
After a claim is processed the plan sends an Explanation of Benefits, called an EOB. The EOB shows the amount billed, the amount the plan allowed, what the plan paid and what you owe. An EOB is not a bill.

## Why a claim may be denied
Common reasons include a service that is not covered, a missing prior authorization, an out-of-network provider, or missing information. The EOB lists the reason for the denial.

## What to do about a denied claim
Check the reason on your EOB, ask your provider to correct any billing error, and file an appeal if you disagree. See the Appeals and Grievances Guide for deadlines.
